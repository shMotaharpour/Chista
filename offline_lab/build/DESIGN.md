# tile_dp — the daily state-action graph, and the DP over it

## Model

- **State** = the tile at **day start** (hour 0): `NONE | WEED | PLANT | ANIMAL |
  EMPTY_COOP | EMPTY_PASTURE`, carrying `(crop, age, consec, fert_left, yield_units)` for a
  plant and `(animal, age, unfed, care_bank, yield_units)` for an animal.
- **Edge** = one daily action chain run by the workers, then idle to the next day start. The
  record is `from, to, chain, entity, cost[], produce[]`.
- **`cost` and `produce` are two 18-wide vectors and are never netted.** `cost` is the inputs
  a chain spends plus labour hours; `produce` is the harvested units of the tile's standing
  product plus the fertilizer a collection picked up. Wheat and fertilizer appear on both
  sides — `FEED` spends wheat, `WATER+HARVEST` makes it — and they are priced by different
  vectors (produce at prices, cost at wages), so netting them would lose that.
- **`entity`** is the entity that parameterises the chain's constructive op (`PLANT`,
  `BUILD_COOP`, `BUILD_PASTURE`, `PLACE`); otherwise the state's own. 0 = none.
- Neither money nor the day is in the artifact: money is a DP input, the day a DP dimension.

## What a day can be

The chains are generated by a walk over the tile's KIND, because every action can change it:

| kind | actions |
|---|---|
| `NONE` | `BUILD_COOP`, `BUILD_PASTURE`, `PLANT <crop>` |
| `EMPTY_COOP` | `PLACE <goose>`, `DIG` |
| `EMPTY_PASTURE` | `PLACE <cow\|sheep>`, `DIG` |
| `WEED` | `DIG` |
| `PLANT` | `FERTILIZE`, `WATER`, `HARVEST`, `DIG` |
| `ANIMAL` | `FEED`, `CARE`, `COLLECT_FERTILIZER`, `HARVEST` |

`PLANT` makes the tile a plant, so the rest of the day may fertilise and water it; `BUILD_*`
makes it a structure, so `PLACE` may fill it; `DIG` makes it bare, so a bare-tile action may
follow. Three rules keep the set canonical rather than combinatorial:

- **Order.** `PLANT > FERTILIZE > WATER > HARVEST`, and for animals
  `FEED > CARE > COLLECT_FERTILIZER > HARVEST`. The engine does not care about the animal
  order; a fixed one is what stops the same day being listed twice. The order holds inside a
  kind-episode and starts again when the kind changes.
- **Once per tile-life.** An op is done once per tile life, and a kind change starts a new
  life: after a `DIG` or a harvest that clears the tile, `PLANT` and `WATER` may happen again.
- **One restart per day.** A tile may be dug and replanted, but a walk that replants and
  harvests and replants is a loop, not a plan, so only one kind change to `NONE` is allowed.

`DIG` is the first op of a chain or comes right after `HARVEST`, and `BUILD_*` needs a bare
tile — which is why changing a barn into a coop goes through `DIG` first.

## Rules the engine imposes

- **A crop left dry on its planting day is a weed by the next day.** A chain that plants
  waters AFTER the plant; water before it belongs to the previous crop and does not count.
- **A harvest leaves a different tile behind per crop.** A one-shot crop is cleared by its
  harvest, so the same day may plant again. An ongoing crop keeps its plant, so a planting
  after the harvest is refused and the tile turns to a weed.
- **A dose does not carry over.** Fertilising the old crop, harvesting it and replanting
  leaves the new plant unfertilised.
- **`PLACE` only works in the species' own structure**, and the structure is named by the op
  (`BUILD_COOP` / `BUILD_PASTURE`), never taken from the entity.
- **`care_bank` is capped** at the species' `max_held`: any larger bank is
  decision-equivalent, because the first production consumes it down to the same place.
- **Market purchases land one turn before the op that needs them**, and the purchase itself
  costs no labour hour.

## Pruning

1. **No-op sweep** — an edge that lands back on the node it started from and produces
   nothing is dropped, whether or not it spends an hour (a 2-hour `DIG+BUILD` self-loop is
   not a decision the DP needs). The **idle day is the exception** (#84): the empty chain
   IS the decision to decline, so it stays even where a day cannot move the tile — bare,
   weed, empty coop, empty pasture. A node that cannot decline has a floor under its value,
   and the DP is forced to spend on a tile it wants to leave alone.
2. **Dominance** — for edges with the SAME next state, `e1` dominates `e2` iff
   `cost(e1)[r] <= cost(e2)[r]` for every resource AND `produce(e1)[r] >= produce(e2)[r]` for
   every resource, with at least one strict component. Compared component by component and
   **never netted**: one wheat is not one melon, a carrot seed is not a wheat seed, and one
   collected fertilizer is not nothing. Only the dominated edge goes.

## The DP over the graph

The graph is day-invariant and position-invariant: every tile, on every day, faces the same
state-action graph. One backward sweep therefore prices the whole board — no per-tile solve
and no per-day rebuild.

```
V_30(s) ≡ 0                                     # the season ends with no liquidation
V_d(s)  = max over e out of s of  produce(e)·p_d − cost(e)·w_d + V_{d+1}(next(e))
```

The terminal row is the season's own end, not a modelling choice: shed goods are worth
nothing at the last day. The argmax is not stored; plans are recovered forward, for the tiles
we own, and the tiles walk in lockstep — one gather and one segmented max per day, because a
per-tile slice pays full dispatch cost. Ties break to the lowest edge index, so a plan is
reproducible run to run.

Two contracts travel with the graph:

- **R006 — non-negativity.** Dominance pruning preserves the optimum only while `p >= 0` and
  `w >= 0` componentwise. A negative component makes a pruned edge the true optimum and the
  DP silently wrong, so it is checked on entry.
- **No empty edge slices.** `np.maximum.reduceat` returns the element *at* the index for an
  empty group, a plausible wrong number instead of an error, so every state must own at least
  one out-edge. Checked at construction.

## The artifact

Chain ids are **positions** in the table that produced them, so the graph and the chain table
are one build and carry one stamp: the registry, the engine, the day length and the key
layout. A load compares them and refuses a pair that does not match, rather than decoding the
ids into other chains.
